# Engineering design process

## Problem and users

The student project focused on preparedness, accessible notification and information sharing around earthquakes. Users considered in the report included school communities and people with sensory or mobility-related accessibility needs.

## Research and requirements

The report describes literature review, discussions with disaster-response personnel, interviews with earthquake survivors and observation of preparedness activities. These activities informed multimodal alerts, a wearable sensor concept, a doorway counter and preparedness screens. Participation in discussions or drills is not described here as agency certification or operational deployment.

## Prototype design and construction

The team combined Arduino/NodeMCU controllers, BMI160 motion sensing, low-cost RF communication, optical pulse sensing, infrared temperature sensing, GPS and two ultrasonic doorway sensors. Enclosures were designed around prototype electronics. The package contains an original ceiling enclosure mesh and photographs of the assembled units.

## Test and observation

The report's classroom test used a manually shaken table and a wearable worn by one student in a ten-student drill. This is a functional demonstration. No raw packet-loss experiment, seismological calibration record, biomedical reference comparison or independent field trial was recovered.

## Evaluation and learning

The recovered sources make integration decisions visible: timed RF signalling, multiple sensing modalities, human-oriented alerts and remote interface concepts. Source inspection also reveals limits around blocking loops, radio pin assignments, location validity and doorway sequencing. The 2026 maintenance logic makes selected failure modes explicit and testable without claiming that these changes were part of the award-winning 2025 implementation.
